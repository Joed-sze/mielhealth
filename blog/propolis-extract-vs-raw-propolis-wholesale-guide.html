Propolis Extract vs. Raw Propolis: Understanding the Difference for Wholesale Buyers

Published: July 28, 2026  |  11 min read  |  By HANGZHOU AADON TRADING CO., LTD.

Executive Summary

  • Clean propolis, refined propolis, and propolis powder serve different market needs and price points.
  • Refined propolis is produced by purifying clean propolis through solvent extraction and concentration, yielding a high-purity solid product ready for direct use.
  • Chinese propolis (poplar type) is characterized by high flavonoid content and strict heavy metal standards, making it ideal for bulk B2B sourcing.
  • Brazilian green propolis is known for Artepillin C and commands premium pricing, while European propolis shares similarities with Chinese propolis.
  • The right choice depends on your target application, regulatory requirements, budget, and supply chain needs.

1. What Is Clean Propolis?

Clean propolis is mechanically processed propolis that has been cleaned to remove debris, excess wax, and hive impurities while retaining the natural resinous matrix. It is the most common form of propolis exported from China for bulk B2B orders. With a propolis content of 55-60%, it offers a balanced combination of bioactive compounds and cost efficiency for buyers who either use it directly in formulations or refine it further.

Clean propolis contains the full spectrum of natural propolis constituents — flavonoids, phenolic acids, terpenes, and essential oils — within a solid, block-like form that is easy to store, transport, and handle.

Key Point: Clean propolis (55-60% purity) is the most cost-effective option for buyers who have in-house purification capabilities or who use propolis as a raw ingredient in their own extraction and manufacturing processes.

2. What Is Refined Propolis?

Refined propolis is produced by further purifying clean propolis to remove waxes, debris, and insoluble materials, resulting in a highly concentrated product with 95% or higher propolis content. The purification process typically involves solvent extraction (using food-grade ethanol), filtration, and concentration, followed by solvent removal to produce a solid, refined product.

Refined propolis is the preferred choice for buyers who need a standardized, high-potency ingredient ready for direct use in supplements, cosmetics, and pharmaceuticals without additional in-house processing.

2.1 Purity Levels and Grades

Refined propolis is typically graded by its total propolis content. Premium grades reach 99% purity and are used in pharmaceutical applications where consistency and minimal excipient content are critical. Standard refined propolis at 95-98% purity is suitable for most dietary supplement and cosmetic applications.

Quality Note: Higher purity does not always mean higher efficacy. The total flavonoid content and specific marker compounds (such as pinocembrin in Chinese propolis or Artepillin C in Brazilian propolis) are often more important indicators of quality than propolis content alone.

2.2 Processing Methods

The most common purification method is ethanol extraction followed by solvent recovery. Food-grade ethanol (70-95% concentration) dissolves the bioactive resins while leaving waxes and insoluble materials behind. The extract is then concentrated and the ethanol is removed, yielding a solid refined propolis product.

Some manufacturers also offer supercritical CO2 extraction, which uses carbon dioxide under high pressure to extract bioactive compounds without solvent residue. This method produces an ultra-pure product (95%+) that commands premium pricing in pharmaceutical and high-end cosmetic markets.

3. What Is Propolis Powder?

Propolis powder is typically made by spray-drying refined propolis onto a carrier substrate such as maltodextrin, starch, or gum arabic. The result is a free-flowing powder that is easy to handle, measure, and incorporate into capsules, tablets, and dry-mix formulations.

Standard propolis powder contains 10-30% actual propolis content (the remainder is carrier). Higher-concentration powders (up to 60% propolis content) are available but require specialized carriers and spray-drying technology.

5. Why Origin Matters: Chinese, Brazilian, and European Propolis

Propolis composition varies dramatically depending on the botanical sources available to bees in different geographic regions. For wholesale buyers, understanding these differences is essential for matching the right propolis type to your target market and application.

5.1 Chinese Propolis (Poplar Type)

Chinese propolis is primarily sourced from poplar, birch, and pine trees in temperate regions. It is characterized by high total flavonoid and total phenolic acid content — often higher than Brazilian propolis on a per-gram basis. The characteristic marker compound is pinocembrin, a flavonoid with documented antimicrobial and anti-inflammatory properties.

China enforces strict quality standards for propolis, with a lead limit of ≤1.0 mg/kg (more stringent than Brazil's ≤2.0 mg/kg standard). Chinese propolis is well-suited for buyers seeking high-volume, cost-effective sourcing for supplement manufacturing, cosmetic formulations, and general health products.

B2B Advantage: China's large-scale beekeeping industry and established processing infrastructure make it the most reliable source for bulk propolis orders with consistent quality and competitive pricing.

5.2 Brazilian Green Propolis

Brazilian green propolis is collected from Baccharis dracunculifolia (alecrim do campo), a plant native to Brazil's tropical regions. Its signature compound is Artepillin C (3,5-diprenyl-4-hydroxycinnamic acid), which has been extensively studied for its biological activity. Brazilian propolis also contains elevated levels of terpenes and prenylated phenylpropanoids not found in poplar-type propolis.

Research confirms that Brazilian and Chinese propolis share only 8-27% compositional overlap, meaning they are chemically distinct products rather than interchangeable grades. Brazilian green propolis commands premium prices (often several times the cost of Chinese propolis) and is positioned in the high-end supplement and cosmetic markets, particularly in Japan and select European countries.

5.3 European Propolis

European propolis is also poplar-type, sharing a similar chemical profile with Chinese propolis. The primary difference lies in volume and price — European production is limited relative to demand, making it less competitive for bulk buyers. Many European supplement manufacturers source clean propolis from China for cost efficiency while marketing finished products under European brands.

6. How to Choose the Right Type for Your Business

Selecting the right propolis product depends on four key factors: your application, target market, regulatory environment, and budget.

For Dietary Supplement Manufacturers

If you produce capsules or tablets, refined propolis or propolis powder is the practical choice. Refined propolis provides standardized potency that simplifies formulation and label claims. Propolis powder (with maltodextrin carrier) flows easily into encapsulation equipment and provides consistent dosing. For premium positioning, consider supercritical CO2 refined propolis with verified flavonoid content.

For Cosmetic and Skincare Formulators

Cosmetic applications benefit from refined propolis that can be dissolved into ethanol or propylene glycol carriers for easy incorporation into formulations. Clean propolis can also be used if you have in-house purification capabilities for custom formulations.

For Food and Beverage Producers

Propolis powder is typically the best option for functional foods and beverages due to its dispersibility and ease of handling. Ensure the carrier (usually maltodextrin) is compatible with your product matrix and labeling requirements.

For Buyers with Extraction Facilities

If your company operates extraction equipment, purchasing clean propolis blocks (55-60% purity) offers the lowest cost per unit of active material. This approach also gives you full control over the extraction solvent and process parameters.

9. Common Buyer Questions

Q1: Can I switch from Brazilian to Chinese propolis in my existing formulation?

Not without reformulation. Brazilian and Chinese propolis have fundamentally different chemical compositions with only 8-27% overlap in key compounds. If your product is positioned around Artepillin C (specific to Brazilian green propolis), switching to Chinese propolis would require reformulation and revised marketing claims. However, if your product markets general propolis benefits such as flavonoid content or immune support, Chinese propolis may be a viable alternative with appropriate COA verification.

Q2: Which purification method yields the highest quality refined propolis?

Supercritical CO2 extraction preserves the broadest spectrum of bioactives without solvent residue, but it is also the most expensive. Ethanol extraction followed by solvent recovery offers the best balance of purification efficiency, cost, and regulatory acceptance for most applications. The key quality indicators are total flavonoid content, low heavy metal levels, and consistent batch-to-batch composition — regardless of the specific purification method used.

Q3: How do I verify the flavonoid content claimed on a COA?

Request the test method used (typically UV spectrophotometry or HPLC) and ask for a chromatogram if using HPLC. Reputable suppliers will provide third-party lab reports from accredited laboratories. For large orders, consider arranging independent testing through SGS, Eurofins, or Intertek.

Q4: Is Chinese propolis accepted in the EU and US markets?

Yes, provided it meets the respective regulatory requirements. For the EU, propolis is regulated as a food supplement and must comply with EU Regulation 1881/2006 for contaminants. For the US, propolis is generally recognized as safe (GRAS) for use in dietary supplements, but FDA registration and compliance with cGMP are required for finished products. Always verify that your supplier can provide COAs meeting your target market's standards.

Q5: What is the shelf life difference between clean propolis and refined propolis?

Clean propolis can remain stable for 3-5 years when stored in a cool, dry place away from direct sunlight. Refined propolis typically has a 2-3 year shelf life, while propolis powder is stable for approximately 2 years.
